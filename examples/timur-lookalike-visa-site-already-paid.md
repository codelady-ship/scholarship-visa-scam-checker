# Example: Timur, a lookalike visa-appointment site (already paid)

> **Fictional case.** The domain below is invented for illustration and is not a real site. In a real session the skill opens the embassy's own page to see which booking site it links to.

## Prompt

> "I couldn't find appointments for my student visa, so I booked on vfs-visa-appointment-priority.com. They charged me €120 for a 'priority slot' by card and asked me to log in with my VFS password to 'link' the booking. Now I have no confirmation. What do I do?"

## Output

> **Next action:** call your bank now about the €120 card payment.

```
VERDICT: Likely scam
Red flags:
• "priority slot" for €120 — VFS says it doesn't sell slots or priority access
• Domain vfs-visa-appointment-priority.com is not the booking site the
  embassy links to
• Asked for your VFS password — official sites never need you to share it
• No confirmation after payment
What's normal: book only through the visa centre site linked from the
embassy's official page; the appointment itself is not sold by third parties.
Safe next step: Open the embassy's site yourself and follow its booking link.
Report: the visa centre's "report fraud" page; your bank; police/cybercrime unit.
```

```
ALREADY PAID / SHARED
1. Call your card issuer now (number on the card): "I was tricked into this
   payment" — ask for a block and chargeback.
2. Change your VFS password and any account using the same password; turn on 2FA.
3. Save screenshots, the URL, the receipt and the date.
4. Report it to the police/cybercrime unit and to the visa centre.
5. Ignore anyone offering to "recover" the €120 for a fee — that's a second scam.
```
