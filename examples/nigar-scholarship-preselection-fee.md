# Example: Nigar, a "pre-selected" scholarship with a processing fee

> **Fictional case.** The sender, phone number and wording are invented. In a real session the skill opens the official programme page and links it; programme rules can change, so always re-check the current page.

## Prompt

> "I got this on WhatsApp from +44 7xxx xxx xxx. Is it real?
>
> *'Congratulations! You have been PRE-SELECTED for the Stipendium Hungaricum Scholarship 2027 (fully funded). To secure your seat, pay the processing fee of USD 150 within 24 hours to our coordinator. Send passport scan + payment receipt to stipendium.hungaricum.office@gmail.com. Limited seats!'*"

## Output

```
VERDICT: Likely scam
Red flags:
• "pay the processing fee of USD 150" — SH applications are free
• "within 24 hours", "Limited seats!" — pressure
• Unsolicited WhatsApp + a gmail.com address, not the official domain
• Asks for a passport scan before any official process
What's normal: SH applications are free and run through the official
application portal; messages come via the portal's Messages section —
https://stipendiumhungaricum.hu (fraud warning: apply.stipendiumhungaricum.hu/news)
Safe next step: Don't pay or send documents. Log in to the official portal
yourself, or ask the programme via the contact on stipendiumhungaricum.hu.
Report: WhatsApp → Report + block; forward to the programme's official
address; national cybercrime unit/police.
```

> **Next action:** don't reply and don't pay. Report the number in WhatsApp, then block it.

This message matches a known pattern of fake "pre-selection" offers. The real programme says applying is free and that nobody can buy you a place.
