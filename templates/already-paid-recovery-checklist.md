# Already Paid or Shared Data: Recovery Checklist

Act fast. Banks and payment services can sometimes stop or reverse a payment only in the first hours or days.

## In the first hour
- [ ] **Stop paying.** Don't send "one last fee" to unlock a refund, visa or scholarship.
- [ ] **Call your bank or card issuer** using the number on your card or the bank's official site. Say: "I was tricked into this payment. Please block, recall or start a chargeback."
- [ ] **Money transfer service** (Western Union, MoneyGram, etc.): call their official line and ask to stop the transfer if it hasn't been collected.
- [ ] **Gift cards:** call the card issuer (number on the back of the card) and give them the card numbers and receipt.
- [ ] **Crypto:** report the transaction to the exchange you used. Recovery is rare, so don't pay anyone who promises it.

## If you shared login details or documents
- [ ] Change the password on that account and on any account that uses the same password
- [ ] Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA)
- [ ] If you shared a passport or ID copy: tell the issuing authority / police if asked, and watch for loans, SIM cards or accounts opened in your name
- [ ] If you shared card details: ask the bank to cancel and reissue the card

## Within 24–48 hours
- [ ] Collect evidence (see `report-checklist.md`)
- [ ] File a police / cybercrime report and keep the reference number; your bank may need it
- [ ] Report the account to the platform where you met the scammer
- [ ] Tell the real organisation that was impersonated (contacts from its official site)

## Watch out for
- [ ] **Recovery scams:** "agencies", "lawyers" or "hackers" who contact you and promise to get your money back for an upfront fee. Legitimate help (bank, police) doesn't charge you to recover stolen money.
- [ ] Follow-up messages from the same scammer under a new name.

## Look after yourself
Being scammed is not your fault. These schemes are run by professionals and target people under deadline pressure. Talk to someone you trust, or your university's support services.

*Not legal advice. For large losses, ask your bank, the police or free legal aid what applies in your country.*
